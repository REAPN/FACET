Diagnostic Criteria
===================

A. Within one two-week period, five or more of the symptoms below are present and are a change from how the person functioned before. One of them must be item 1 (depressed mood) or item 2 (loss of interest or pleasure). Symptoms clearly due to another medical condition are not counted.
1. Low mood for most of each day on most days, either reported by the person (for example feeling sad, empty or hopeless) or seen by others (for example looking tearful). In children and adolescents the mood can be irritable instead.
2. Much less interest in, or enjoyment of, nearly every activity, for most of each day on most days, by the person's own account or as others observe it.
3. Marked weight loss without dieting, or weight gain (for example more than five percent of body weight within a month), or an appetite that is lower or higher on most days. In children, not gaining weight as expected also counts.
4. Sleeping too little (insomnia) or too much (hypersomnia) on most days.
5. Agitation or slowing of movement on most days, visible to others and not only a private sense of restlessness or of being slowed.
6. Tiredness or lack of energy on most days.
7. Feeling worthless, or guilt that is excessive or unwarranted (possibly to a delusional degree), on most days, beyond simply blaming oneself or feeling guilty about being ill.
8. Reduced capacity to think clearly or to concentrate, or trouble making decisions, on most days, by the person's own account or as others observe it.
9. Repeated thoughts about death (more than fear of dying), repeated thoughts of suicide without a specific plan, a specific plan for suicide, or a suicide attempt.
B. The symptoms cause clinically meaningful distress, or impairment in social life, at work or in another important area of life.
C. Neither a substance, through its effects on the body, nor another medical condition accounts for the episode.
D. Schizoaffective disorder does not account better for at least one major depressive episode, and the episode is not layered on top of a schizophrenia spectrum or other psychotic disorder (such as schizophrenia, schizophreniform disorder or delusional disorder).
E. There has never been a manic or hypomanic episode, unless every such episode was caused by a substance or by another medical condition.
